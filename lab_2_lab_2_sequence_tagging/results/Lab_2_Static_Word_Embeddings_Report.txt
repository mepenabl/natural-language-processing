LAB 2 — STATIC WORD EMBEDDINGS

This laboratory focused on understanding and applying static word embeddings using different NLP libraries such as Gensim, NLTK and SpaCy.

The first part of the lab explored pre-trained embeddings, specifically the GloVe “glove-wiki-gigaword-300” model. Different operations on embeddings were performed, including:

- Loading pre-trained word vectors
- Accessing words and vector representations
- Computing cosine similarities
- Finding semantically similar words
- Evaluating embeddings using intrinsic evaluation benchmarks

For the similarity task, the embeddings successfully identified semantically related words. For example:
- “people” was associated with words such as “others”, “many”, and “thousands”
- “women” was associated with “men”, “girls”, and “female”

This demonstrates that the embedding space captures semantic relationships between words.

The intrinsic evaluation on the MEN benchmark produced strong correlation scores between human similarity judgments and cosine similarities computed from embeddings:

- Pearson Correlation ≈ 0.73
- Spearman Correlation ≈ 0.74

These results indicate that the pre-trained embeddings preserve meaningful semantic information.

The second part of the lab focused on training custom Word2Vec embeddings using a COVID-19 social media dataset.

Several preprocessing techniques were applied:
- Lowercasing
- Punctuation removal
- Stopword removal
- Lemmatization using SpaCy

The preprocessing stage reduced noise and vocabulary sparsity while preserving semantic information.

After preprocessing, a custom Word2Vec model was trained on the dataset. This allowed the generation of vector representations specialised for COVID-related terminology and social media language.

Overall, the lab provided practical experience with:
- Static word embeddings
- Semantic similarity
- Intrinsic evaluation metrics
- Text preprocessing pipelines
- Training custom Word2Vec models

The experiments showed how embeddings can effectively capture semantic and contextual relationships between words while also demonstrating the importance of preprocessing and evaluation in NLP workflows.
